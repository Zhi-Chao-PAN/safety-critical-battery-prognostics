## K. Fairness Validation

A critical question for fair comparison is whether post-processing alone—without physics-informed training—suffices to guarantee physical consistency across diverse neural architectures. This investigation addresses potential methodological bias: if simple post-processing techniques can achieve zero violation rates for all models, then PINN's advantage might be attributed to post-hoc correction rather than intrinsic architectural properties. To test this hypothesis, we apply identical post-processing pipelines (exponential moving average with α=0.15 followed by running-minimum enforcement) to all six models under 50% Gaussian noise conditions (200 cycles, seed=42), ensuring that any performance differences arise from model-intrinsic characteristics rather than post-processing implementation details.

For these architectures, the monotonicity assumption embedded in running-minimum enforcement aligns with their intrinsic prediction patterns, resulting in improved numerical accuracy alongside zero violation rates. Conversely, the "post-processing hostile" group (TCN, CNN1D) experiences substantial RMSE degradation (+35.8%, +21.3%), revealing fundamental incompatibility between their internal prediction dynamics and the monotonicity assumptions of post-processing. TCN's 35.8% RMSE penalty—the highest among all models—indicates that its dilated convolution architecture generates prediction patterns that fundamentally conflict with running-minimum enforcement, forcing the post-processor to introduce large corrections that destroy numerical fidelity. CNN1D's 21.3% penalty similarly reflects architectural mismatch, where local convolution operations produce high-frequency prediction variations that are incompatible with global monotonicity constraints. These results demonstrate that post-processing effectiveness is not universal but depends critically on model-intrinsic prediction characteristics.

The key insight emerging from this analysis is that PINN's advantage cannot be evaluated solely through synthetic data performance, where post-processing can achieve comparable violation rates at lower computational cost. On synthetic data with simple monotonic degradation patterns, LSTM+post-processing achieves superior RMSE (0.0825 Ah vs. 1.3573 Ah for PINN), suggesting that physics-informed training introduces unnecessary complexity for idealized scenarios. However, this advantage disappears on real-world battery data, as demonstrated in Section V.D where PINN achieves 0% violation rate across all 6 CALCE batteries, while LSTM achieves only 48% violation rate despite identical post-processing. This discrepancy highlights that synthetic data's simplified noise structure and monotonic degradation patterns fail to capture the complexity of real battery aging, which includes non-monotonic capacity recovery, temperature-dependent degradation knees, and sensor calibration drift.

The practical implication for BMS deployment is that post-processing provides a universal safety net but with model-dependent reliability and accuracy trade-offs. For safety-critical applications requiring guaranteed performance across diverse operating conditions, PINN's integrated physics-informed defense offers superior robustness because it enforces physical constraints during training rather than as post-hoc corrections. The post-processing hostile behavior observed for TCN (+35.8% RMSE penalty) and CNN1D (+21.3% RMSE penalty) demonstrates that not all models can "freely" achieve zero violation rates—some architectures pay substantial accuracy costs that may be unacceptable for precision-critical applications. PINN's moderate 6.9% RMSE penalty under post-processing reflects its inherent physical consistency, where additional constraints provide marginal incremental benefit rather than fundamental correction.

In summary, while post-processing enables all models to achieve zero violation rates on synthetic data, the effectiveness varies dramatically across architectures, with some models experiencing unacceptable RMSE degradation. PINN's true advantage emerges in out-of-distribution generalization scenarios, where physics-informed training provides reliability guarantees that post-processing alone cannot ensure. The combination of fair synthetic data validation (this section) and real-world performance verification (Section V.D) establishes PINN as the superior choice for safety-critical battery prognostics, balancing synthetic data efficiency with real-world robustness.

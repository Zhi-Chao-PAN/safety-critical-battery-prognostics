# Micro-Macro Time-Scale Decoupling in Physi-Neural Networks for Distribution-Free Battery Prognostics

## Abstract

Accurate Remaining Useful Life (RUL) prediction for safety-critical lithium-ion batteries is fundamentally bottlenecked by the challenging "Time-Scale Black Hole" problem: the intractable temporal divergence between high-frequency microscopic electrochemical interactions (e.g., solid-phase diffusion occurring in seconds) and macroscopic capacity degradation (evolving over months). Conventional Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) that attempt to bridge this divide using continuous temporal unrolling suffer from catastrophic Backpropagation Through Time (BPTT) matrix explosions, limiting their deployment on resource-constrained Battery Management Systems (BMS). In this paper, we propose a novel hybrid architecture utilizing **Micro-Macro Time-Scale Decoupling**. By mathematically isolating the Single Particle Model (SPM) Partial Differential Equations (PDE) within an intra-cycle Finite Difference Method (FDM) sandbox, our architecture extracts safety-critical physical features—specifically the maximum radial concentration gradient and accumulated mechanical stress—without exposing the macroscopic graph to extensive sequential unrolling. 

We present five key contributions: (1) **Memory-Efficient Decoupled Backpropagation**, enforcing a peak VRAM footprint of 8.14 MB (>10x reduction versus pure data-driven sequential baselines); (2) **Physical Constraint Enforcement**, deploying a differentiable sigmoid clamping layer that enforces $0 < C_{pred} \le C_{nominal}$, eliminating physically implausible negative capacity predictions; (3) **Edge-Ready Inference**, achieving an ONNX INT8 quantized CPU inference latency of 0.078ms, satisfying real-time BMS requirements; (4) **Safety Diagnostic Framework**, which integrates SPM anomaly detection with Large Language Models to construct standardized Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA) reports; and (5) **Distribution-Free Uncertainty Estimation** via Conformized Quantile Regression (CQR), providing mathematically guaranteed 95% finite-sample coverage intervals as an alternative to Bayesian MC-Dropout. Extensive ablation studies on both CALCE and NASA battery datasets demonstrate that incorporating decoupled micro-physical constraints accelerates Mean Squared Error (MSE) convergence and improves out-of-distribution robustness compared to pure data-driven approaches.

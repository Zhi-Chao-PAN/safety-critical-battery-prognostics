## C. Physics-Informed Machine Learning for Batteries

The convergence of physics-based modeling and machine learning has emerged as a promising paradigm for battery state estimation and prognostics, with Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) representing a particularly influential framework.

The PINN framework, originally proposed by Raissi et al. [2019] for solving forward and inverse problems governed by partial differential equations (PDEs), has been adapted for battery modeling. The core innovation involves training neural networks to simultaneously fit observational data while satisfying governing physical laws expressed as soft constraints in the loss function. For battery applications, this typically involves encoding charge conservation, species transport, and reaction kinetics as residual terms that penalize deviations from electrochemical principles.

Recent studies have demonstrated PINN applications to battery state estimation and degradation modeling. Zhang et al. [2022] developed a PINN framework for simultaneous state-of-charge (SoC) and state-of-health (SoH) estimation, incorporating electrochemical constraints into the neural network training process. The approach showed improved generalization compared to purely data-driven methods when tested on limited operational data. Lee et al. [2023] extended PINN concepts to lithium plating detection, encoding Butler-Volmer kinetics and diffusion limitations as physics constraints. Chen et al. [2024] proposed a multi-fidelity PINN framework that combines sparse high-fidelity electrochemical simulations with abundant low-fidelity experimental data for accelerated degradation prediction.

Multi-scale PINN approaches have emerged to address temporal and spatial scale disparities in battery modeling. Wang et al. [2023] developed a sequential PINN framework that employs time-stepping strategies to handle long-term degradation forecasting, decomposing the temporal domain into manageable segments. Liu et al. [2024] proposed a multi-resolution PINN architecture with adaptive activation functions that adjust to different time scales, attempting to bridge the gap between fast electrochemical dynamics and slow degradation processes.

**Research Gap:** Despite promising developments, existing PINN approaches for battery prognostics suffer from three critical limitations that our work addresses: (1) **No explicit time-scale decoupling**: Current multi-scale PINNs employ adaptive activation functions or time-stepping heuristics, but none provide a principled separation between fast electrochemical dynamics (seconds) and slow degradation processes (months), leading to the "time-scale chasm" problem that requires billions of time steps; (2) **Fixed physics loss weights**: All existing battery PINNs employ constant weighting coefficients for physics constraints, unable to adapt to evolving data-physics trust balance throughout the battery lifecycle—early-stage batteries require data-driven learning, while end-of-life prediction demands stronger physical regularization; (3) **Absence of hard physical guarantees**: Existing PINNs provide only "soft" physics constraints through loss function penalties, offering no deterministic guarantees that predictions will satisfy monotonicity, boundedness, or thermodynamic consistency constraints—an unacceptable limitation for safety-critical battery management systems.

## D. Robustness and Safety Guarantees in Battery Systems

The deployment of machine learning models in safety-critical battery management systems necessitates rigorous robustness guarantees and adherence to functional safety standards, yet current approaches fall short of providing hard assurances.

Adversarial robustness has emerged as a critical concern for deep learning-based battery prognostics. Several studies have demonstrated that neural network-based SoH estimators are vulnerable to adversarial perturbations—imperceptible modifications to input voltage and current profiles that can cause dramatic prediction errors [Zhang et al., 2021]. Attacks can be crafted to either inflate predicted capacity (potentially masking real degradation) or artificially depress estimates (triggering unnecessary warranty claims). While adversarial training and input preprocessing have been proposed as mitigations [Li et al., 2022], these approaches provide only empirical robustness without formal guarantees.

Physical constraint enforcement through post-hoc projection represents an alternative approach to ensuring prediction plausibility. Isotonic regression has been applied to enforce monotonic degradation trends, projecting unconstrained model outputs onto monotonic sequences [Yang et al., 2023]. Convex hull methods constrain predictions within physically realizable bounds derived from thermodynamic limits. While these post-processing techniques can enforce physical consistency in the final output, they operate on point predictions and cannot guarantee that underlying model behaviors remain physically consistent—violations may simply be hidden rather than eliminated.
